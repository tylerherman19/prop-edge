PROP EDGEMETHOD
THE BOARD ACCURACY METHOD

The whole machine in one paragraph: a collector wakes up on a schedule, pulls this week's player-prop lines from two free market feeds and this week's player projections from two free model feeds, matches them player by player and stat by stat, subtracts, and ranks the board by the size of the disagreement. No paid data, no logins, no scraped logins - every feed is an open door the publisher's own app walks through.

THE LINES

WHAT THE MARKET EXPECTS

FanDuel. The sportsbook's own web client reads anonymous JSON for every game: passing, rushing and receiving props with the real line and both prices (the -114/-114 style numbers). When the board shows FanDuel, you are looking at an actual posted number with juice attached.

PrizePicks. The pick'em app's public board: one flat line per player and stat, no odds. Useful as a second opinion on where the market sits, and it covers far more players than the sportsbook page does.

THE MODELS

WHAT THE PROJECTIONS EXPECT

Sleeper. The fantasy app's open API serves weekly stat-level projections - yards, catches, touchdowns, attempts - for every rostered player. Refreshed through the week.

ESPN. ESPN's fantasy API is anonymously readable and carries per-week projections in the same shape. The two models disagree with each other plenty; the Accuracy page tracks which one lands closer.

THE FOUR NUMBERS

LINE, ESTIMATE, GAP, HIT CHANCE

Every prop on the board carries four numbers. The line is the market's number. The estimate is what the model expects the player to do. The gap is the distance between them, in the stat's own units. The hit chance is how often a gap that size actually held up across the 2025 season.

Your advantage means the estimate sits above the line - room the market is not pricing. Vegas advantage means the estimate sits under the line - the book's number looks safe. Neither is a command. It is a measurement of disagreement, not a prediction of the future and not betting advice. Models are wrong constantly - the Accuracy page shows exactly how wrong, in plain numbers.

Props that join cleanly to at least one model make the board. Props that don't - defenses, kickers, some deep backups - are counted in the data note and left out rather than guessed at.

THE HIT CHANCE

HOW IT'S COMPUTED, IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Take every 2025 projection the model made for one stat - say receiving yards - and measure how far each one landed from what the player actually did. That gives a miss curve: the full shape of how the model gets that stat wrong. When a new prop shows the estimate 12 yards above the line, the hit chance is the share of 2025 where the model's miss was smaller than 12 yards. If the model missed by less than that 61% of the time, the card says 61%.

Each stat gets its own curve for each model, and one for the average of the two models. Combo stats like rush + receiving yards get their own curves built from paired weeks. Stats with no 2025 curve - longest reception, for example - show no hit chance rather than a made-up one. The curves refresh every week as the new season's games get graded.

THE PLUMBING

WHERE IT LIVES

A scheduled job on GitHub pulls the feeds, computes the board, and writes it into a Supabase database. This site reads the latest snapshot straight from the database when you open it. Lines move during the week; the snapshot time is in the black bar up top, and every snapshot is kept so edges can be graded after the games.

Built on free public endpoints. Like every open gate, any of these feeds can close or change shape without notice - if a feed dies, the data note on the board will say which one.
