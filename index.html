PROP EDGEWK -
THE BOARD ACCURACY METHOD

Loading the board - pulling the latest lines and projections.

Every prop is priced both ways, like a prediction market. The two percentages come from the player's own game log - recency-weighted, shrunk toward his position's league average when he's played few, then calibrated on 2025 results. Model pick marks the side his log favors. The estimate underneath is this week's Sleeper/ESPN projection, a separate signal - when the two disagree, the card says so and you make the call.

Only six stats carry a percentage - rushing yards, rush attempts, receiving yards, receptions, targets, and rush+rec yards. Those are the ones with enough 2025 props to fit and test a curve. Passing stats, interceptions and touchdown props show a dash: the same test said the model was no better than a coin flip there, so the board prints nothing rather than a number it cannot stand behind. The Accuracy page shows that test.

Read the percentages as calibrated to a proxy. No free archive of 2025 sportsbook lines exists, so the calibration was fitted against Sleeper's projections standing in for the line. Sleeper runs high on rushing, so the fitted curve shades toward the under - which is why far more props read "Vegas advantage" than the other way. That tilt is a property of the proxy, not a read on this week's market.

Edge is the default sort: hit-chance confidence times the model-vs-line disagreement, measured in the player's own spread. It rewards size of disagreement, not agreement between the two signals, so check the card. Hit% and Gap sorts are there when you want one signal at a time.

A big gap is a disagreement, not a guarantee. Nothing here is betting advice - it is arithmetic on free public data.
