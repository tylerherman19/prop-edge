THE BOARD ACCURACY METHOD

Two claims get tested here. One: which free projection model lands closer to what players actually do. Two: when a model disagrees with the market line, does the model side win. The first has a full 2025 season of evidence. The second started counting the week this site went live - the record below fills in as games finish.

HEAD TO HEAD, 2025 SEASON

PROJECTIONS VS ACTUALS

Every 2025 regular-season week, every player projected for a real role, each model's number checked against what the player actually did. In plain terms: out of all those checks, how close did the model usually land?

THE HIT% MODEL, TESTED

2025 WALK-FORWARD, HELD-OUT WEEKS 13-18

The board's over/under percentages come from a different model: a player's own game log versus the line. It is scored here the only way that means anything - fit on the early weeks, tested on weeks it never saw, and compared against the one baseline that is hard to beat: always predict how often the over hit in the training weeks. Brier score, lower is better. A stat only reaches the board if this test says it earned a number.

THE LIVE RECORD

EDGE VS MARKET, GRADED AFTER GAMES

Every snapshot line gets kept. After each week's games, the grader checks the biggest edges against what players actually did and posts the scorecard here.

WHAT THIS CAN'T SAY YET

READ THIS PART

Historical market lines are not in any free feed, so the 2025 test grades projections against real outcomes - not against 2025's lines. The honest statement that data supports: "this model is usually within X of the truth." The statement it cannot yet support: "this edge would have beaten 2025's market." That second claim starts accumulating from the week the collector went live, one graded week at a time.

Generated from free public data. Misses are measured in the stat's own units - yards, catches, touchdowns.
